Infant and under-five mortality in Minas Gerais, 2022 Census

This vignette estimates infant (1q0) and under-five (5q0) mortality in the state of Minas Gerais from the summary birth history of the 2022 Brazilian Census, using the Brass method (Hill 2013).

library(childmortality)

Choosing the model life table family

The indirect method relies on a model life table family, which sets the age pattern of mortality under age 5. To choose it, we compare pairs of 1q0 and 4q1 from Brazilian vital statistics with the patterns of the model families.

# 1q0 and 4q1 from Brazilian vital statistics
obs <- list("Br 2022" = c(0.01246, 0.0023),
            "Br 2023" = c(0.01273, 0.0022),
            "Br 2024" = c(0.01257, 0.0022))
plot_mlt(obs)

Zooming in on the families closest to the observed points:

plot_mlt(obs, families = c("South", "Latam", "FarEast", "Chilean"))

The South family (Princeton) is the most adequate for Brazil in 2022 and is used below.

0. Data: Minas Gerais

MinasGerais is a strand of the public-access microdata of the 2022 Census: women aged 15-49 living in the state, with the variables needed by the package (see ?MinasGerais). Minas Gerais is often described as a synthesis of Brazil. Its regions range from the poorer, semi-arid north, close to the Brazilian Northeast, to the more developed south and Triângulo Mineiro, close to São Paulo, so the state’s averages are close to the national ones.

The variables keep the IBGE codes, so they are cleaned first:

  • age: the public-access file gives only the five-year group (P0180, 4 = 15-19, …, 10 = 45-49). Any age inside the group works, because the method only uses these groups. Here we take the midpoint, 5 * P0180 - 3.
  • children dead: the census asks for children born alive (P0340) and children alive (P0370), so children dead are the difference. P0370 is blank for women with no children, who have no dead children.
  • births in the last 12 months: P0400 is 1 = yes, 2 = no and blank for women with no children.
mg$age <- 5 * mg$P0180 - 3
mg$cd <- ifelse(mg$P0340 == 0, 0, mg$P0340 - mg$P0370)
mg$births <- as.integer(mg$P0400 %in% 1)

Every function takes the weight (P0110) and, optionally, the sex filter. The file only has women, so the filter is shown for completeness.

w <- "P0110"
female <- list(var = "P0150", female = 2)

The mean age at maternity is only used by the UN families (Brass-Palloni-Heligman variant). The South family is a Princeton family (Brass-Trussell variant, with d(i) = 0), so M does not enter the estimates below.

As usual with the Brass method, the estimates from women aged 15-19 are biased upwards, because children of very young mothers face higher mortality. Those from the older groups refer to a more distant past and are more affected by recall errors. The estimates from women aged 20-34 are the most reliable.
